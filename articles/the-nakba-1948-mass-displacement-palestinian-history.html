The Nakba of 1948: Mass Displacement and Palestinian Historical Memory

By Super Admin 👁 2,423 Views 📖 1 min read
Al-Nakba ("The Catastrophe") refers to the mass expulsion and displacement of over 750,000 Palestinian Arabs from their ancestral towns and villages during the 1948 War. More than 500 Palestinian villages were depopulated or destroyed. Cities such as Jaffa, Haifa, Lydda, Ramle, and West Jerusalem saw vast numbers of residents forced into exile across the West Bank, Gaza Strip, Jordan, Lebanon, and Syria. The Nakba remains the foundational event of modern Palestinian national identity, commemorated annually on May 15.
